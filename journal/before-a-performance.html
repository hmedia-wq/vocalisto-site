PERFORMANCE · AUGUST 8, 2026

Prepare the task. Preserve the voice.

The goal before a performance is dependable coordination and confidence—not a second performance backstage.

Work backward from the set

Consider the first entrance, tessitura, style, diction, dynamic extremes and any passages that require unusual coordination. The warm-up should prepare those demands without rehearsing every difficult moment at full intensity.

Use a compact sequence

  1. Check physical and vocal state.
  2. Establish easy breath and gentle vibration.
  3. Move through comfortable patterns before approaching performance range.
  4. Touch the relevant register transitions and consonant patterns.
  5. Sing one or two representative phrases below performance intensity.
  6. Leave enough quiet time before the first entrance.

Plan around the environment

Travel, dry rooms, noise, temperature, soundcheck and schedule can change the voice more than an extra scale can fix. Protect speaking volume, allow time and avoid competing with a loud room.

Do not chase reassurance

Repeating the highest or hardest note can become an anxiety loop. If coordination is present, save the full artistic commitment for the stage.

A simple ruleThe warm-up should leave you more available than when you started. If it creates fatigue, reduce demand or stop.
